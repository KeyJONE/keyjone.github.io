Stabilized unified multi-physics framework for methane hydrate production

Z.-Q. Tang, J. Yang, Q. Zhang, and W.-Q. Xu

Computers and Geotechnics, 202:108567, 2027.

  • Manuscript

The safe and efficient extraction of methane hydrate reservoirs relies heavily on the accurate prediction of intricate interactions among thermal (T), hydraulic (H), mechanical (M), and chemical (C) responses. However, standard finite element simulations for methane hydrate typically lack a unified framework capable of accommodating the various coupling modes (e.g., TH, HM, THM, THMC) required across diverse engineering scenarios. Moreover, they often suffer from numerical instabilities, particularly in near-undrained conditions and convection-dominated flows. To address these challenges, this study proposes a unified multi-physics coupling framework designed to balance production enhancement strategies with reservoir stability. The main contributions of this framework include: (1) the architecture allows for dynamic spatiotemporal switching and coupling of different multi-physics schemes, tailoring the simulation to specific extraction phases and distinct reservoir strata. (2) an advanced hybrid stabilization strategy is incorporated to eradicate numerical artifacts. Specifically, the bubble-enhanced edge-based smoothed finite element method (bES-FEM) is employed to satisfy the inf-sup condition and eliminate pressure oscillations, while the Streamline Upwind Petrov-Galerkin (SUPG) technique is integrated to suppress instabilities in convection-dominated transport. (3) the explicit–implicit scheme is further implemented to efficiently handle the strong non-linearities inherent in hydrate dissociation. Following rigorous validation against benchmarks, the framework is then applied to field-scale methane extraction models. The results demonstrate the method’s proficiency in capturing complex THMC phenomena, revealing the thermal field as a dominant controlling factor and elucidating the positive coupled effects of lower-strata depressurization and heating on reservoir behavior. Consequently, this work provides a reliable computational tool for investigating the complex multi-physics mechanisms underlying methane hydrate production.
